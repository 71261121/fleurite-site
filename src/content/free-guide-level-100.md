## PART 1: THE TRUTH ABOUT SHRINKING (What They Don't Tell You)

### You Weren't Born This Way

There was a moment—maybe many moments—when you learned that your needs weren't safe.

Maybe someone important got angry when you reached out. Maybe they disappeared when you showed emotion. Maybe they made you feel like a burden for simply existing with feelings and desires.

So your nervous system learned the rule: *"If I'm smaller, quieter, less needy—I survive."*

**But here's what's happening in your body right now:**

Your nervous system is in **chronic survival mode**. It's flooding your system with cortisol (stress hormone) every time he:
- Takes longer than usual to text back
- Says he "needs space"
- Pulls away emotionally
- Gets quiet during conflict

Your body reads these moments as threats because it learned early: *"Emotional distance = abandonment = death."*

So you do the only thing that felt safe as a child: **You shrink.**

You watch his face for signs. You apologize for having needs. You say yes when you mean no. You suppress your voice until you become so small he might forget you're even there.

**The exhausting part? This never actually keeps you safe. It just trains him that he can get everything from you while giving nothing back.**

---

### The Cost of Shrinking (What's Happening to Your Body Right Now)

Every time you suppress a need, your nervous system registers it as a threat you couldn't manage. So it goes on higher alert.

This is why you experience:

**Physical symptoms:**
- Heart racing when his notification arrives (or doesn't)
- Inability to sleep (your nervous system is too activated)
- Chest tightness during conflict
- Nausea when you consider expressing a boundary
- Exhaustion that sleep doesn't fix

**Emotional symptoms:**
- Hypervigilance (constantly scanning for signs he's pulling away)
- Shame (blaming yourself for his distance)
- Rage (at yourself, at him, at the situation)
- Numbness (you've dissociated from your own needs for so long)
- Desperation (the thought of him leaving triggers survival panic)

**Behavioral symptoms:**
- Obsessive phone checking
- Over-functioning (doing everything to "earn" his love)
- People-pleasing (saying yes to everything)
- Seeking reassurance constantly
- Abandoning your own interests/friends

**This isn't weakness. This is your body in survival mode trying to prevent abandonment.**

---

### The Pattern That's Destroying You Both

Here's how the cycle deepens:

**Week 1:** He pulls away → You chase → He feels controlled → He pulls harder

**Week 2:** You chase harder → You shrink more → He gets more comfortable → You start disappearing from your own life

**Week 3:** You're now living for his approval → He's learned he can withdraw and you'll rearrange your world → Your nervous system is completely dysregulated

**Week 4+:** You're numb. You've forgotten what you actually want. He's comfortable. And you're dying slowly.

**The truth:** He's not doing this to you. He's doing what works. And what works is: You chase, he wins, you lose, repeat.

**But the even deeper truth:** You're not staying for love anymore. You're staying for survival. For the nervous system's false belief that *if you just try hard enough, you'll finally be safe.*

You won't. Because safety can't come from shrinking. Safety only comes from **honoring yourself.**

## PART 2: THE 3-DAY NERVOUS SYSTEM RESET (Micro-Level Psychology)

### How This Works

Your nervous system has learned a lie: *"I'm only safe when I'm invisible."*

This reset teaches it the truth: *"I'm safe when I listen to myself and act on what I know."*

**Important:** This isn't about "manifesting" or wishful thinking. This is neuroscience. When you honor your own needs, your vagus nerve (the nerve that controls your nervous system state) shifts from **sympathetic** (threat/survival) to **parasympathetic** (rest/safety).

Repeat this cycle daily for 3 days, and your nervous system starts to believe: *You're worth protecting.*

---

### DAY 1: THE MIRROR (Noticing Without Shame)

**Morning (5 minutes):**

Before anything else, sit down with water. Your nervous system needs hydration after a night of processing.

Write down:
- **One moment today** where you wanted to speak up but didn't
- **One situation** where you said yes when you meant no
- **One feeling** you had but didn't voice
- **One need** you abandoned to keep the peace

Example:
- "He texted saying he was tired. I wanted to ask for a 15-minute call but didn't because I didn't want to bother him."
- "He asked me to reschedule my plans to see him later. I said yes immediately even though I had plans with myself."
- "I felt hurt when he made a joke about something I'm sensitive about. I laughed instead of saying it bothered me."
- "I needed reassurance that he cared, but I didn't ask because I didn't want to seem needy."

**Why this works:** Your nervous system has been on autopilot. It's shrinking without your conscious permission. The first step is making it **conscious**. Awareness is the first layer of change.

**Throughout the day:**

Notice (without judgment) each time you:
- Bite your tongue when you want to speak
- Agree to something you don't want
- Suppress an emotion to keep him comfortable
- Abandon your own needs for his

Just notice. Write it down if you can. Your nervous system needs to know: *You're paying attention.*

**Evening (3 minutes):**

Read back what you wrote. **Feel it.** Don't dismiss it. Don't make excuses for him. Just let yourself feel:

"I've been shrinking."

Say it out loud. Your body needs to hear it from you.

---

### DAY 2: THE BOUNDARY (One Small Act of Self-Honoring)

**Morning (1 minute):**

Drink water. Say this to yourself:

*"Today, I'm going to honor one need. Not dramatically. Just one real thing."*

**Pick ONE of these (whatever feels most relevant to your situation):**

**Option A - The Delayed Response:**
If he texts you, wait 1 hour before responding (unless it's an emergency). During that hour, do something that makes YOU feel good. Drink coffee. Walk outside. Scroll your phone for fun (not obsessively checking his profile).

Why: Your nervous system learns "I don't exist to respond on his timeline. I have my own timeline."

**Option B - The Small Request:**
Text him or tell him something you normally wouldn't say:

*"Hey, I've been stressed and I'd feel better if we checked in before bed. Can we do that?"*

OR

*"I know you're busy tomorrow, but I was hoping to see you. What's your availability?"*

OR

*"Something's been bothering me and I'd like to talk about it when you have time."*

Why: Your nervous system learns "My needs are worth naming. They're not shameful."

**Option C - The Boundary:**
If he asks you to do something that doesn't feel right, say:

*"I'm going to think about that"* or *"That doesn't work for me"* instead of automatic yes.

Why: Your nervous system learns "I get to decide what happens to my own time/body/energy."

**Option D - The Reassurance:**
When anxiety spikes, instead of seeking reassurance from him (which trains you to depend on his validation for safety), give it to yourself:

*"I'm safe right now. My nervous system is scared, but that's okay. I can handle this."*

Then do something grounding: 5 deep breaths, hands in cold water, feet on ground, 5-4-3-2-1 senses exercise.

Why: Your nervous system learns "I can self-soothe. I don't need him to feel safe."

**Throughout the day:**

Notice how it feels to honor this one need. Notice any resistance (guilt, fear, shame). This is your nervous system saying:

*"Wait—are we SURE this is safe? This is new."*

Yes. It's safe. Keep going.

**Evening (2 minutes):**

Write down:
- What you did
- How it felt
- Any shame/guilt that came up (important data)
- What your nervous system learned

---

### DAY 3: THE INTEGRATION (Making It Real)

**Morning (1 minute):**

Drink water. Remember what you learned yesterday.

**Throughout the day:**

Do the same boundary again, but this time add ONE word: Notice how different it feels the second time.

**Evening (5 minutes - The Crucial Part):**

This is where it becomes real.

Call or text ONE trusted person (friend, family, therapist—anyone who has your back).

Tell them out loud:

*"I realized something about myself. For a long time, I've been shrinking. Making myself small. Abandoning my own needs to keep someone comfortable. And for the last 3 days, I've been noticing it. And I've been practicing honoring my needs instead. I wanted to tell you because saying it out loud makes it real."*

**Why this is critical:**

When you say it out loud to another person, your nervous system registers it as TRUE. Your body believes it more. Your brain creates new neural pathways. You're no longer in shame-silence. You're in truth-speaking.

The person doesn't have to fix it or validate you. They just have to hear it. The power is in the saying.

**After the conversation:**

Write down how it felt to say it.
