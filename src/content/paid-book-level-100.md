# SECTION 1: THE FOUNDATION

## CHAPTER 1: THE AVOIDANT CYCLE (And Why You're In It)

### Understanding Avoidant Attachment

Avoidant attachment is not a choice he's making to hurt you. It's a nervous system pattern.

**How it forms:**

As a child, his emotional needs probably went unmet. A parent was:
- Emotionally unavailable
- Punitive when he showed feelings
- Unpredictably present/absent
- Cold, critical, or dismissive

So his nervous system learned: "Needing anyone = danger."

His survival rule became: *"I'm only safe when I'm completely independent. Feelings are weakness. Intimacy is suffocation."*

Now, every time intimacy increases (you get closer, you ask for more, you show vulnerability), his nervous system sounds the alarm:

**THREAT DETECTED: ENGULFMENT**

His body floods with adrenaline. His brain tells him: *"Get away. Now."*

This is not personal rejection of you. This is his nervous system protecting him from what feels like obliteration.

**But here's what you need to know:** His nervous system protection is not your job to manage.

### Understanding Your Attachment Pattern (The Anxious-Avoidant Dance)

You probably have anxious attachment. Which means your nervous system learned something different:

*"I'm only safe when I'm needed. When I'm small and accommodating. When I can somehow control his availability through effort and shrinking."*

This creates the perfect storm:

**He withdraws to feel safe.**

**You chase to feel safe.**

**The more you chase, the more he withdraws.**

**The more he withdraws, the more desperately you chase.**

You're both trying to survive. And you're both making each other's nervous systems worse.

## CHAPTER 2: Why You Shrink & What It Costs You

### The Nervous System Science

When he pulls away, your nervous system reads it as:

**ABANDONMENT DETECTED: IMPLEMENT SURVIVAL PROTOCOL**

Your amygdala (threat detector) floods your system with cortisol and adrenaline. Your prefrontal cortex (rational brain) goes offline. You're operating from pure instinct.

The instinct is: *"Make yourself useful. Make yourself invisible. Do whatever keeps him from leaving."*

So you:
- Text less (don't want to bother him)
- Ask for less (don't want to be needy)
- Want less (maybe if you don't need anything, he'll stay)
- Become less (your interests disappear, your voice shrinks, you become a pale version of yourself)

### The Cost of Chronic Shrinking

**To your body:**
- Disrupted sleep (cortisol keeps you activated)
- Digestive issues (your gut nervous system is dysregulated)
- Tension/pain (muscles locked in protection mode)
- Weakened immune system (stress hormones suppress immunity)
- Aging accelerated (chronic stress damages cells)

**To your psyche:**
- Loss of identity (who are you outside of him?)
- Depression (suppressed needs create hopelessness)
- Anxiety disorder (your nervous system is permanently vigilant)
- Shame (you blame yourself for his withdrawal)
- Rage (at him, at yourself, at the situation)

**To the relationship:**
- Real intimacy becomes impossible (you're performing, not being)
- Trust erodes (he doesn't really know you anymore)
- Resentment builds (you're keeping score of your sacrifices)
- Passion dies (fear-based connection is not erotic)
- The cycle deepens (he's more comfortable with less of you, so he pulls more)

## CHAPTER 3: The Binary Truth About Relationships

### There Are Only Two Outcomes

**Outcome 1: He wakes up. His nervous system gets safe. He does the work. Intimacy becomes possible.**

For this to happen:
1. He has to admit there's a problem (many don't)
2. He has to want to change (not just want to keep you—actually want to change his nervous system)
3. He has to get help (therapy, coaching, intentional work)
4. He has to follow through (not just talk, actually do it differently)

If he's doing all four? Real change is possible. It's slow (nervous systems take years to rewire). But it's possible.

**Outcome 2: He doesn't wake up. He keeps doing what works. You stay in pain or you leave.**

He likes the dynamic where:
- You chase, he wins
- You shrink, he gets comfortable
- You do the emotional labor, he gets the reward
- He gets all your energy with none of the responsibility

If this is happening, no amount of shrinking, no perfect script, no sacrifice will change it.

Because the system works for him.

### The Question That Changes Everything

**"Is he showing any signs of being willing to change?"**

Not "Do I love him enough to make this work?"

Not "Should I try harder?"

Not "Is it my fault he's distant?"

**Is HE willing to change?**

This is the question that determines everything.

And you can't know the answer until you ask directly.

Which is exactly what the scripts teach you to do.

# SECTION 2: THE 47 SCRIPTS

## How to Use These Scripts

### Before You Use Any Script

**Read this:**

These scripts work because they do three things:
1. **Name reality** (without theory, without blame, without emotion)
2. **State your boundary** (without apology, without shrinking)
3. **Give him information** (so he can choose what to do next)

The scripts are NOT:
- Manipulation tactics
- Ways to make him change
- Tests of his love
- Punishment

The scripts ARE:
- Communication tools
- Clarity generators
- Nervous system recalibration
- Truth-speakers

### The Micro-Level Psychology Behind Why They Work

Each script does something specific to your nervous system:

**When you speak your truth:**
- Your vagus nerve shifts from fight/flight to rest/digest
- Your prefrontal cortex (rational brain) comes back online
- You stop operating from desperation
- You show your nervous system: "I can handle this. I don't need to collapse."

**When you set a boundary:**
- You teach your nervous system: "I get to decide what happens to me"
- You signal: "I have standards for how I let people treat me"
- You retrain your amygdala: "This threat is manageable"

**When he responds:**
- You get data (is he willing or not?)
- Your nervous system learns what happens when you speak up
- You can make an informed decision (not a fear-based one)

## SCRIPT SET 1: When He Pulls Away (Slow Replies, Sudden Distance)

**Scenario:** He goes from texting all day to taking 6+ hours. He's withdrawn. You're spiraling.

**What's happening in his nervous system:** Intimacy triggered his fear. He's creating distance to feel safe.

**What's happening in your nervous system:** You're reading his distance as abandonment. Your amygdala is screaming. You want to chase to make it stop.

**WRONG RESPONSE:**
"Why are you ignoring me? You always do this when things get real. This is so frustrating."

*Why it fails:* He feels attacked → His nervous system goes into defense mode → He pulls away harder → You chase harder → Cycle deepens.

---

### SCRIPT 1A: The Gentle Reality Check (Use This First)

**Send:**
> "I noticed things shifted between us. I'm feeling anxious about it. Are you okay, or do you actually need space right now?"

**Why it works:**
- You name the pattern without accusation ("things shifted" = neutral observation)
- You own your feeling ("I'm feeling anxious" = your responsibility)
- You ask directly (no games, no guessing)
- You give him an out ("Are you okay?") so he doesn't feel attacked

**What his responses mean:**

**If he says: "I'm just busy" or "Nothing's wrong"**

*What's true:* He's uncomfortable with the conversation. His nervous system is defensive.

*Next move:*
> "Okay, I believe you. I also know when something shifts for me. So either there's something you're not saying, or my nervous system is being weird. Either way, can we check in tonight? I need to know we're okay."

*Why:* You're not backing down from your reality. You're also giving him a chance to be honest.

**If he says: "I do need space"**

*What's true:* He's being honest. His nervous system is activated by intimacy.

*Next move:*
> "I can give you space. I need to know: Is this a few hours, or are we talking about something bigger? So I can understand what I'm in."

*Why:* You're not demanding. You're asking for information. This tells you: Is he taking a break or is he checking out?

**If he says: "I don't know" or gives vague answer**

*What's true:* He's avoiding. He can't name what he's feeling.

*Next move:*
> "That's okay not to know. But I need to know: Can you let me know when you figure it out? Because I can't sit in the not-knowing."

*Why:* You're setting a boundary. "I can't sit in the not-knowing" = "Uncertainty in relationships makes me dysregulated. I need clarity."

**If he doesn't respond at all**

*What's true:* He's choosing silence as his response. This is data.

*Next move (after 24 hours):*
> "I'm going to assume you need time right now. I'm going to step back. Let me know if that doesn't work for you."

*Why:* You're not sitting around waiting for him to decide your fate. You're making a decision. This shows your nervous system: "I don't need him to tell me what to do. I can make choices."

---

### SCRIPT 1B: When He's Still Distant After 24 Hours

> "I know opening up is hard. And I've noticed you're pulling away. I'm not here to make this harder. I just need to know: Is this something we're working through together, or is this goodbye? Because I need clarity more than I need anything else right now."

**Why it works:**
- You validate his difficulty ("I know opening up is hard")
- You name the pattern without blame ("you're pulling away")
- You clarify your position ("I'm not here to make this harder")
- You ask the real question ("Is this goodbye?")
- You state your boundary ("I need clarity")

**What happens next depends entirely on his answer.**

**If he commits to working through it:** Great. Move to Script Set 5 (Rebuilding With Boundaries).

**If he says "I need time to think" but doesn't give a timeline:** He's avoiding. Come back with Script 1A again. You're not trying to force him. You're just refusing to sit in limbo.

**If he confirms it's over:** You have your answer. Move to Script Set 7 (Leaving With Dignity).

## SCRIPT SET 2: When He Shuts Down Mid-Conversation

**Scenario:** You're trying to talk about something that matters. He gets quiet. Withdrawn. "Fine, whatever."

**What's happening:** He's flooded. His nervous system registered your concern as criticism. His body is in shutdown (freeze response).

**What NOT to do:**
- Yell at him
- Keep pushing
- Say "You never talk to me"
- Criticize his shutdown
- Give up entirely

**All of these make it worse.**

---

### SCRIPT 2A: The Nervous System Reset Pause

**Say:**
> "I can see you're shutting down. I don't want to do this while you're flooded. Can we take 15 minutes and come back to it? I'm not going anywhere. This is important to me, and I want us both to be able to talk about it."

**Why it works:**
- You name what's happening (he's shutting down) without judgment
- You offer a break (let his nervous system regulate)
- You clarify your intent ("I'm not going anywhere" = "This isn't punishment")
- You state importance ("This matters to me, so we will come back")

**What happens:**
- He either agrees (good, his nervous system is listening)
- He says no (his nervous system is in protection mode, more work needed)
- He leaves/goes silent (he's in full shutdown, wait longer)

**If he says no or leaves:**

Wait 30-60 minutes. Then:

> "Hey. I'm not mad. I just need to know: When can we talk about this? Because avoiding it makes me more anxious, not less. So let's pick a time."

**Why:* You're not forcing. You're setting a boundary. "Avoiding makes me more anxious" = "I need resolution, not silence."

---

### SCRIPT 2B: When You're Actually Ready to Talk

**Start with:**
> "I know opening up is hard. I'm not trying to attack you or criticize you. I'm trying to be honest about what I'm feeling because I care about this. Can you hear me?"

**Why it works:**
- You acknowledge his difficulty
- You clarify intent ("not trying to attack")
- You state your position ("I'm being honest because I care")
- You ask permission ("Can you hear me?")

**Then say what you actually need:**

"When [specific thing], I feel [specific feeling]. And I need [specific thing]. Can we figure this out?"

Examples:
- "When you get quiet during hard conversations, I feel alone. And I need you to stay present even when it's uncomfortable. Can we try that?"
- "When you say 'I don't know' to everything, I feel dismissed. And I need you to at least try to name what you're feeling. Can you do that?"
- "When you make jokes about something I'm sensitive about, I feel hurt. And I need you to take that seriously. Can we talk about what's off-limits?"

**Why this structure works:**
- **Specific trigger** (not "you always" but "when [exact thing]")
- **Your feeling** (you own it, he doesn't have to fix it)
- **Your need** (concrete, doable)
- **Open question** (gives him agency)

**If he says YES:**

> "Thank you. I know this isn't easy for you. It's not easy for me either. But it matters that we can do this."

**Why:* You're normalizing difficulty. You're showing gratitude. You're honoring the work.

**If he says NO or doesn't answer:**

> "Okay. So you're not willing to [specific thing]. That tells me something important. We need to talk about what that means for us."

**Why:* You're not accepting silence. You're naming what his refusal means. This brings you to the Binary Question.

## SCRIPT SET 3: When He Ghosts or Disappears

**Scenario:** Everything was fine. Then he vanishes for 8+ hours with no explanation. No response to your message.

**What's happening:** His nervous system got triggered. He fled. He's in avoidant shutdown mode.

**What you're thinking:** He's done with me. He's with someone else. I did something wrong.

**What's actually true:** He's dysregulated and running.

---

### SCRIPT 3A: The First Check-In (6 Hours In)

> "Hey. I'm noticing you disappeared. Just checking if you're okay or if something's happening between us that we need to talk about."

**Why it works:**
- You name the pattern ("you disappeared")
- You express concern ("checking if you're okay")
- You ask directly ("is something happening")
- You're not accusatory

**Wait 12 more hours.**

**If still nothing:** Send Script 3B.

---

### SCRIPT 3B: The Clarity Boundary (24 Hours In)

> "I'm going to take your silence as an answer. If something's wrong, I'm open to talking about it. If you're done, I deserve to know that. Either way, I deserve clarity. I'm giving you one more day to respond. After that, I'm making decisions about what this means."

**Why it works:**
- You're naming the consequence of ghosting (his silence has meaning)
- You're giving him one more chance
- You're taking your power back ("I'm making decisions")
- You're not waiting indefinitely in limbo

**What happens next:**

**If he responds with an excuse:**
> "I appreciate you reaching out. Here's what I need to know: Is this a one-time thing or should I expect you to disappear when things get hard? Because I can't keep doing the uncertainty."

**Why:* You're getting to the real question: Can he handle conflict/difficulty or does he flee?

**If he responds saying he's done:**
> "Okay. Thank you for being honest. That's hard, but I respect it. I'm going to take some time. Reach out if anything changes, but I'm not waiting."

**Why:* You're honoring the clarity. You're not begging him to stay.

**If he never responds after Script 3B:**
> You have your answer. He's shown you: When things get hard, he disappears. That's the information you needed.

## SCRIPT SET 4: When He Says You're "Too Much"

**Scenario:** "You're too needy. Too emotional. Too demanding. I can't deal with this."

**What he means (if he's being real):** "Your needs feel threatening to my nervous system. Intimacy is hard for me."

**What he means (if he's avoiding):** "I don't want to do the work. I want you small and manageable."

**This is crucial. This is where you find out which one it is.**

---

### SCRIPT 4A: The Reframe

> "When you say I'm too much, what you're saying is my needs feel threatening to you. That's about your nervous system, not about me. What I need to know is: Are you willing to work on that, or is this a dealbreaker for you?"

**Why it works:**
- You reframe the problem (it's not you, it's his dysregulation)
- You ask the real question (is he willing to work?)
- You don't accept the frame that you're broken
- You set a boundary (this matters)

**What his response tells you:**

**If he says: "I'll work on it"**

*What you should hear:* "Great. Let's define what that looks like."

> "I'm glad. Because I need to see actual change, not just talk. Here's what I need: [2-3 specific things]. Can you commit to that?"

**Examples:**
- "I need you to ask how my day was and actually listen. Not look at your phone."
- "I need you to not call me needy when I ask to see you. I need you to say yes or explain why you can't."
- "I need you to tell me the truth about what's happening instead of disappearing."

*Why specific:* Vague promises ("I'll be better") mean nothing. Specific commitments can be evaluated.

*Write it down.* He should see it. You should see it. This creates accountability.

**Check in after 2 weeks:** "I've noticed [specific change] happening. Thank you. I'm also noticing [specific thing] hasn't changed. Can we address that?"

*Why:* Real change shows up in behavior, not words. If it's only words, you know.

**If he says: "I don't know" or "I'll try"**

*What this means:* He's not committing. He's avoiding.

> "Okay. But I need to know: Is this something you actually want to work on? Because 'I'll try' tells me maybe not. And if you're not interested in changing this, I need to know that too. Because I need to make decisions about what's best for me."

**Why:* "I'll try" is not a commitment. It's a way to buy time without actually promising anything. You're calling it out.

**If he says: "No" or "This is just who I am"**

*What this means:* He's not willing. This is the information you needed.

> "Okay. So you're not willing to work on this. I hear that. I need to take some time to decide what that means for me."

**Why:* You're not trying to change him. You're deciding if this is acceptable to you. (Spoiler: It's probably not.)

---

### SCRIPT 4B: The Second Conversation (If He Promised Change But Nothing Shifted)

> "I notice I said I needed [specific thing] and we agreed you'd work on it. But it hasn't actually changed. I need to understand: Are you actually willing to do something different, or are we pretending?"

**Why it works:**
- You name the specific failure (not "things are still bad" but "you agreed to X and didn't do it")
- You call the difference between talk and action
- You ask directly if he's actually willing
- You don't let him hide in vagueness

**This conversation answers the Binary Question: Will he actually change?**

**If YES:** He gets real. He acknowledges the failure. He does something different (maybe gets therapy, reads about avoidant attachment, practices).

**If NO:** He makes excuses, gets defensive, gaslights you into thinking you asked too much, or disappears.

**Either answer is valuable. You now know.**

## SCRIPT SET 5: Setting Your First Real Boundary

**Scenario:** You're ready to stop accepting crumbs. You're going to ask for something real.

**What you're feeling:** Fear. Guilt. "What if he leaves?"

**What you need to know:** If he leaves over a reasonable boundary, the relationship was already over. You were just the only one working.

---

### SCRIPT 5A: The Small Boundary (Start Here)

Pick one thing that matters to you:

> "I need to tell you something that's hard for me to say. In [specific situation], I need [specific thing]. Can we talk about that?"

**Examples:**

"In conversations about us, I need you to actually engage instead of shutting down. Can we do that?"

"When we make plans, I need you to follow through unless something legit comes up. Can I expect that?"

"When I'm upset, I need you to ask what's wrong instead of avoiding me. Can you do that?"

"I need you to check in with me during the day, at least once. Can we do that?"

**Why this structure works:**
- You lead with "this is hard for me" (you're being vulnerable)
- You're specific (not "be better" but "do X")
- You're asking for a commitment ("can we do that?")
- You're giving him a chance to say yes or no

**If he says YES:**

> "Thank you. I know this isn't easy for you. But it matters to me, so I'm grateful."

**Why:* You're acknowledging that he stepped up. You're showing appreciation.

*Then:* Actually hold him to it. When he follows through, acknowledge it ("Thank you for texting me today. That meant something."). When he forgets, gently remind him ("Remember we agreed on this?").

**If he says NO:**

> "Okay. So you're not willing to [specific thing]. That tells me something important about what you're willing to offer. I need to think about what that means for me."

**Why:* A NO is data. His unwillingness shows you: This matters to him more than you do.

**If he says "That's too much" or makes you feel guilty:**

This is critical.

> "I hear that it feels like a lot. And I'm not trying to be demanding. I'm trying to tell you what I need to feel secure in this. If my needs are too much for you, that's important information. Because I can't make myself smaller anymore."

**Why:* You're not backing down. You're setting a boundary about your boundary. "I can't make myself smaller anymore" = "This is the bottom."

---

### SCRIPT 5B: The Bigger Boundary (Once He's Proven He Can Handle Small Ones)

Only use this after he's shown willingness with smaller boundaries.

> "I've been thinking a lot about us. And I need to be honest: For this to work long-term, I need [bigger thing]. I need to know: Is this something you can actually commit to, or am I fooling myself?"

**Examples:**

"I need you to actually be present when you're with me. Not on your phone. Not half-here. Fully here. Can you commit to that?"

"I need you to stop ghosting when things get hard. I need us to talk through conflict instead of you disappearing. Can you do that?"

"I need you to introduce me to your friends and family. I need to stop feeling like a secret. Can that happen?"

"I need us to make concrete plans for the future. I can't keep living like this is temporary. Can we do that?"

**Why this matters:**

Bigger boundaries are about the relationship's foundation. If he won't commit to these, the relationship can't actually work.

**If he says YES:**

You're entering territory where he's actually committing to change. Move to Script Set 6 (Rebuilding With Boundaries).

**If he says NO or gets defensive:**

He's shown you: He won't step up. You now have clarity.

## SCRIPT SET 6: The Clarity Conversation (The Most Important Script)

**Use this when the pattern keeps repeating.**

**Use this when you need to know: Will he change or not?**

> "I need to be really honest with you about something. I love you, but I'm in pain. The pattern we're in—where I chase and you pull away—is hurting me. I've tried changing how I respond. I've made myself smaller. I've asked for things and you've said yes but nothing actually changes. And I need to know: Are you actually willing to work on this? Not just say it. Actually do it. Because I can't keep living like this."

**Why this is the most important script:**

This script does three things:

1. **Names the pattern** (chase/pull away) so there's no confusion
2. **Takes responsibility** ("I've tried changing," "I've made myself smaller") so he can't say you're not trying
3. **Asks the real question** ("Are you actually willing?")

**Then wait for his answer.**

Don't fill the silence. Don't soften it. Let him sit with the question.

---

### What His Response Means

**If he says: "Yes, I'm willing"**

> "Great. Because here's what that looks like for me: [list 2-3 specific changes you need to see]. And I need to see actual change, not just effort. Can you commit to that?"

**Examples:**
- "You need to go to therapy and actually do the work. I need to see you applying what you learn."
- "You need to stop disappearing when things get hard. You need to talk to me."
- "You need to stop saying hurtful things when you're triggered."

**Write it down. Review it in 2 weeks. 4 weeks. 8 weeks.**

Ask him: "How do you think you're doing with what we agreed to?"

If he's genuinely trying, you'll see it. If he's not, you'll see that too.

**If progress is real:** Stay. This is worth fighting for.

**If nothing changes despite his promises:** Move to Script Set 7 (Leaving).

**If he says: "I don't know" or "I'll try"**

This is not a yes. This is him buying time.

> "I hear you. But 'I don't know' and 'I'll try' tell me maybe not. And if you're genuinely interested, I need to hear: 'Yes, I'm willing to work on this.' Can you say that?"

**If he says it:** Great, move forward with the commitment conversation above.

**If he can't say it or gets defensive:** He's not willing. See below.

**If he says: "No" or "I don't think I can change"**

Okay. You have your answer. He's being honest.

> "I appreciate your honesty. That's actually a gift. But it means I have to make a choice about what's best for me. I'm going to take some time and figure out what that looks like."

**Move to Script Set 7 (Leaving).**

**If he says: "It's you. You're the problem. I'm not willing to change because there's nothing wrong with me"**

This is blame-shifting. This tells you: He's not capable of accountability right now.

> "I hear you. You don't think there's a problem. But I do. And I can't live in a dynamic where only I'm trying. So I'm going to step back and figure out what's best for me."

**Move to Script Set 7.**

# SECTION 3: THE BINARY QUESTION

## How to Know If He'll Actually Change

### The Three-Question Framework

After Script Set 6 (The Clarity Conversation), ask yourself these three questions:

**Question 1: Is he willing to admit there's a problem?**

- YES = He acknowledges the pattern is hurting you and him
- NO = He blames you, denies the pattern, or says "this is just who I am"

**Question 2: Is he taking action to address it?**

- YES = He's in therapy, reading books, talking to friends, doing inner work
- NO = He says he'll work on it but does nothing, or he thinks just talking about it is enough

**Question 3: Is he showing up differently in the relationship?**

- YES = You're seeing concrete behavior changes (responding faster, engaging in conversations, showing interest)
- NO = Everything is the same, he just promised to change

### What The Answers Mean

**All three YES:** He can change. This is worth staying for. Keep building with boundaries.

**Two YES, one NO:** He's trying but something's missing. Either he's not deep enough in therapy, or he's not motivated enough. Give it 8 weeks of consistent effort. If progress stalls, he's not willing.

**One YES, two NO:** He's not actually willing to change. He's just saying the right things to keep you. Leave.

**All three NO:** He's shown you clearly: He's not interested. Leave immediately.

# SECTION 4: THE CLARITY FRAMEWORK

## Stay With Boundaries (If He's Willing)

### The Deal With Rebuilding

If he said YES to the Clarity Conversation and the Binary Question shows YES-YES-YES, here's what rebuilding actually looks like:

**It's slow.** Nervous systems take 6-18 months to rewire.

**It's not linear.** He'll have setbacks. He'll regress. This is normal.

**It requires both of you.** You can't do his work for him. You can only do yours.

**What you need to do:**

1. **Stop shrinking** - You model what healthy looks like

### The Reality Check (8 Weeks In)

- Is he in therapy and actually engaging?

- Does he acknowledge his patterns?
- Is he willing to be uncomfortable to grow?

**If no to any:** Adjust your expectations. Or leave.

### Why 3 Days Isn't Enough

The 3-day reset teaches your nervous system a new possibility. But your nervous system has 20+ years of evidence that you're not safe.

You need consistent practice to rewire.

### The Daily Practice (Weeks 2-12)

**Every day, do ONE of these:**

**Morning (2 minutes):**
- Breathing exercise (4-count in, 6-count out, 10 times)
- Affirmation: "I'm safe when I honor myself"
- Cold water on face (activates vagus nerve)

**Afternoon (1 minute):**
- Check in with your body: Where am I holding tension?
- Release it: Shake, stretch, breathe

**Evening (3 minutes):**
- Journaling: One boundary I honored today
- One need I expressed
- One way I felt proud

**Weekly:**
- 20-minute walk in nature (regulates nervous system)
- One conversation with someone who makes you feel safe
- One small act of self-care (not bubble baths, I mean actual care: sleep, nutrition, exercise)

**You will regress. You will shrink again. This is normal.**

1. **Notice it without shame** - "I'm shrinking. My nervous system got scared."

3. **Do the 3-day reset again** - Yes, from the beginning
4. **Adjust expectations** - This is not failure. This is healing.
