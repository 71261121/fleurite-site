## The Exact Words to Say + How to Know If He'll Actually Change

## PART 1: UNDERSTAND — Why You Chase & He Pulls Away

### Chapter 1: The Pattern Nobody Warns You About

**Great date. Deep conversation. Real connection.**

Then overnight: Replies slow down. They suddenly "need space." The warmth just... vanishes.

It feels like rejection. It's not.

For someone with avoidant attachment, intimacy triggers their nervous system like a threat. Not because of you. Because of how they learned safety as a child.

**What's happening inside their head:**

Their nervous system learned early: "Needing anyone = loss of control = danger."

As a child, their emotional needs probably weren't met. So they learned the safest rule: *"I'm only safe when I need no one."*

Now, every time you get close—every vulnerability, every ask, every "I need you"—it activates that ancient fear. Their body floods with cortisol. They experience closeness as suffocation.

Their move? Withdraw. Create distance. Make you chase.

**What's happening inside YOUR nervous system:**

You learned a different rule: "If I'm small and perfect, I'll be safe."

So when he pulls away, you panic. Your nervous system reads it as: *"I'm not worthy. I need to fix this. I need to earn back his attention."*

You chase. You apologize for things you didn't do. You shrink yourself more. You ask less. You need less.

And he gets more comfortable. Because now there's no demand on him.

**This is the dance. And it's slowly killing you both.**

---

### Chapter 2: The Cost of Shrinking

**The more you shrink yourself, the more comfortable they get.**
**The more space you give, the more they take.**

You're not building a relationship. You're building their comfort zone where they get everything and give nothing.

**What happens to your nervous system:**

- Chronic anxiety (he might pull away, so you stay vigilant)
- Hypervigilance (scanning his mood, his responses, his distance)
- Loss of self (you've suppressed so much you forgot who you are)
- Deep shame (you blame yourself for his avoidance)
- Panic attacks (his silence triggers your survival response)

**What happens to the relationship:**

- No real repair happens (you're too afraid to name the problem)
- Intimacy dies (safety is required for vulnerability)
- Resentment builds (you're carrying the emotional weight alone)
- The cycle deepens (he pulls, you chase, he pulls harder)

**What he gets:**

- All his space
- All your effort
- None of the responsibility
- You constantly earning back his attention

This isn't love. This is a hostage situation you're financing with your own nervous system.

---

### Chapter 3: The Binary Truth

**There are only two outcomes in this dynamic:**

**1. He wakes up.** His nervous system gets safe enough to choose closeness. He does the work. He becomes available. Real intimacy becomes possible.

**2. He doesn't.** He keeps doing what works. You keep shrinking. The relationship stays in pain, or it ends.

There is no middle ground where you keep shrinking and he suddenly becomes secure. That doesn't exist.

**So the real question isn't "How do I make him change?"**

**The real question is: "How do I know which one is happening so I can make a choice?"**

## PART 2: REWIRE — The 47 Scripts + How to Know If He'll Change

### The Foundation: What Actually Works

Before scripts, you need to understand **why** these work.

Most people panic when he pulls away. They chase. They make it worse.

Here's what actually works: **Clarity without aggression. Boundary without punishment. Honesty without shame.**

These scripts work because they do three things:
1. Name what's actually happening (without theory, without blame)
2. State your need clearly (without apology or shrinking)
3. Give him information (so he can choose what he wants to do)

---

### SCRIPT SET 1: When He Pulls Away (Replies Slow, Suddenly "Needs Space")

**Scenario:** He goes from texting all day to taking 8+ hours. You're spiraling.

**WRONG:** "Why are you ignoring me? This is why I can't trust you."
*Result: He feels attacked. He pulls away harder.*

**RIGHT:**
> "I noticed things shifted. I'm feeling anxious about it. Are you okay, or do you actually need space right now?"

**Why it works:** You name the pattern without accusation. You ask directly. You give him a chance to be honest.

**If he says "I need space":**

> "I can give you space. I need to know: Is this a few hours, or are we talking about something bigger? So I can understand what I'm in."

**Why it works:** You're not demanding. You're asking for information so you can make a conscious choice about what to do next.

**If he doesn't respond to that either:**

> "I'm going to assume you need time right now. I'll reach out tomorrow. Let me know if that doesn't work for you."

**Why it works:** You're taking your power back. You're not sitting around waiting for him to decide your fate. You're making a decision and inviting him to participate.

---

### SCRIPT SET 2: When He Shuts Down Mid-Conversation

**Scenario:** You're trying to talk about something that matters. He gets quiet, withdrawn, or "fine, whatever."

**WRONG:** "You're shutting me out again. You never talk to me."
*Result: He feels attacked and retreats further.*

**RIGHT:**
> "I know opening up is hard. I'm not here to attack you. I'm here because this matters to me, and I want us both to feel heard. What would make this feel safer for you right now?"

**Why it works:** You validate his fear first. Then you name what you actually want: being heard. You ask what would help instead of demanding.

**If he still shuts down:**

> "I can see you're not ready to talk about this right now. That's okay. But I need to know: When can we come back to it? Because we can't just pretend this isn't happening."

**Why it works:** You're not accepting silence as an answer. You're naming that this matters and requires his participation.

**If he says "I don't know":**

> "That's okay not to know right now. What I need from you is: Can you come back to this in the next few days? Yes or no?"

**Why it works:** You're asking for a concrete commitment, not vague promises. This tells you a lot about whether he's willing to work.

---

### SCRIPT SET 3: When He Ghosts or Disappears

**Scenario:** Everything was fine. Then he vanishes for hours/days with no explanation.

**FIRST:** Wait 24 hours. If he reaches out, great. If not:

> "Hey. I'm noticing you disappeared and haven't checked in. I want to know: Are you okay, or is something happening between us that we need to talk about?"

**Why it works:** You're not accusing. You're expressing concern and asking for clarity. This is what a secure person does.

**If he still doesn't respond after 24 more hours:**

> "I'm going to take your silence as an answer. If something's wrong, I'm open to talking about it. If you're done, I deserve to know that too. Either way, I deserve clarity."

**Why it works:** You're telling him the truth: His silence is a choice, and it has consequences. You're not going to sit in limbo.

**If he comes back with an excuse later:**

> "I appreciate you reaching out. I also need to know: Is this a one-time thing, or should I expect that when things get hard, you disappear? Because I can't keep doing the not-knowing."

**Why it works:** You're asking the question: "Is this salvageable or not?" His answer tells you everything.

---

### SCRIPT SET 4: When He Says You're "Too Much"

**Scenario:** "You're too needy. You're too emotional. You're too demanding."

**This one is critical. Listen carefully.**

**WRONG:** "I'm not too much. You're just broken."
*Result: War.*

**RIGHT:**
> "When you say I'm too much, what you're actually saying is my needs feel threatening to you. That's about your nervous system, not about me. And I need to know: Are you willing to work on that, or is this a dealbreaker for you?"

**Why it works:** You're not accepting the frame that you're the problem. You're reframing it as "he has a nervous system issue" and asking if he's willing to address it.

**MOST IMPORTANT FOLLOW-UP:**

If he says yes but never actually changes, come back with:

> "I notice you said you'd work on this, but the pattern hasn't changed. I need to know: Are you actually willing to do something different, or are we pretending?"

**Why this matters:** This is where you find out if he's capable of change or just saying what you want to hear.

---

### SCRIPT SET 5: Setting Your First Real Boundary

**Scenario:** You're ready to stop shrinking. You're going to ask for what you actually need.

**This is where most women get scared.**

**Start small. Pick ONE thing:**

> "I need to tell you something that's hard for me to say. In [specific situation], I need [specific thing]. Can we talk about that?"

**Examples:**

- "...I need you to check in with me at least once during your workday. Can we do that?"
- "...I need you to not take 12 hours to respond unless you tell me why. Can we do that?"
- "...I need you to actually show up when we make plans. Can we do that?"

**Why this works:** You're specific. You're not attacking. You're asking for a concrete change.

**If he says "that's too much":**

> "I hear that it feels like a lot. Can you tell me what specifically feels hard about it?"

Listen to his answer. If it's actually a legitimate reason, you can negotiate. If it's just "I don't want to," you know something important: He's not willing to meet you halfway.

---

### SCRIPT SET 6: The Clarity Conversation (Use This When You Need to Know If He'll Change)

**This is the most important script. Use it when the pattern keeps repeating.**

> "I need to be really honest with you about something. I love you, but I'm in pain. The pattern we're in—where I chase and you pull away—is hurting me. And I need to know: Are you actually willing to work on this? Not just say it, but actually do it? Because I can't keep living like this."

**Then pause. Let him answer.**

**If he says YES:**

> "Good. Because here's what that looks like for me: [name 2-3 specific changes]. Can you commit to that?"

Write it down. Hold him accountable. If he doesn't follow through, bring it up again. If *still* no change, you have your answer.

**If he says NO or "I don't know":**

> "Okay. Then I have to be honest: I can't keep doing this. This is where I need to make a choice about what's best for me."

**And then you actually have to make that choice.**

---

### SCRIPT SET 7: Leaving With Dignity (If That's What You Choose)

**Sometimes the answer is: He won't change, and you're done.**

**WRONG:** Angry explosion, threats, ultimatums he doesn't believe.

**RIGHT:**

> "I've realized something: I can't be the only one working on this relationship. And I deserve someone who chooses me. So I'm going to make a choice for myself: I'm leaving. I'm not doing this to punish you. I'm doing it because I deserve better."

**Why this works:** You're not begging him to change so you can stay. You're naming your boundary and honoring it.

**If he says "please don't go" or "I'll change":**

> "I've heard that before. And I've watched it not happen. I need to believe through action, not words. So I'm going to take space. If you do the real work—therapy, taking responsibility—maybe we can revisit this later. But right now, I need to go."

**Why this matters:** You're not slamming the door. You're leaving it open. But you're also not staying in pain while he "thinks about it."

## The Real Truth About Change

**Here's what determines if someone actually changes:**

1. **They have to admit there's a problem** (many avoidants never do)
2. **They have to want to change** (not just want to keep you—actually want to change their nervous system)
3. **They have to do the work** (therapy, inner work, accountability)
4. **They have to show up differently** (not just talk about it)

**If he's doing all four? He can change.**

**If he's only doing one or two? He won't.**
