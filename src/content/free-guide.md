# Why You Shrink Yourself (And What Your Body Really Needs)

## The 3-Day Reset for Anxious Attachment

## PART 1: Understanding Why You Shrink

### You weren't born doing this.

There was a moment—maybe many moments—when you learned that your needs weren't safe.

Maybe someone important got angry when you needed them. Maybe they disappeared when you reached out. Maybe they made you feel like a burden for simply existing with feelings.

So you learned: *If I'm smaller, quieter, less needy—I'll be safer.*

You learned to watch his face for signs. To apologize before he got upset. To say yes when you meant no. To shrink your wants, your voice, your presence—down, down, down—until you became tolerable.

**But here's what's true:** Your nervous system wasn't broken. It was *injured*.

---

### What happens when you shrink yourself

When you consistently suppress your needs, something shifts. Your nervous system stops believing you can keep yourself safe. It stays on high alert.

That's why:
- Your anxiety spikes when he's distant
- You obsessively check your phone for his messages
- You panic when he doesn't respond in the "normal" time
- You replay conversations looking for what you did wrong
- You feel guilty for even having needs

**This isn't weakness. This is your body trying to survive an unsafe dynamic.**

---

## PART 2: What Your Body Actually Needs

### The 3-Day Reset

Your nervous system needs to relearn one thing: **You're safe when you honor yourself.**

**Day 1: Notice Without Changing**

Before you do anything differently, just observe. For 24 hours, notice:
- One moment where you wanted to say something but didn't
- One time you agreed to something you didn't want
- One instant where you shrunk to avoid conflict

Write them down. Just notice. Don't judge yourself.

**Day 2: Small Expansion**

Pick ONE small boundary to honor:
- If he texts late: Don't respond immediately. Wait 30 minutes.
- If he asks for something you don't want: Say "I need to think about it" instead of automatic yes.
- If you're anxious: Name it to yourself. "My nervous system is scared right now. That's okay."

One small act of honoring yourself. That's it.

**Day 3: The Integration**

Talk to one trusted person about what you noticed. Say out loud:
- "I realized I've been shrinking myself"
- "My nervous system has been in survival mode"
- "I'm learning to honor my own needs"

Saying it out loud makes it real. Your body believes it more when you speak it.

You don't have to untangle this alone. But you do have to start believing: Your needs aren't the problem. The refusal to meet them is.
